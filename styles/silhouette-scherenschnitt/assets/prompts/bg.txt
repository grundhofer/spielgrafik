Style: traditional paper-cut silhouette art (Scherenschnitt) as in 1920s silhouette animation films and shadow theatre, made as a background for a side-view adventure game. Everything is cut with a fine knife from flat matte paper in exactly four flat tones and laid flat on top of each other: pure white #FFFFFF paper for the empty sky and the empty sea, light grey #BDBDBD paper for the far distance, dark grey #6E6E6E paper for the middle distance, pure black #000000 paper for the foreground. Every shape is one solid flat tone with crisp knife-cut edges: no gradients, no shading, no texture, no outlines, no highlights, no drop shadows, no blur, no glow. Depth is shown only by the tone of the paper: the farther, the lighter. Fine filigree knife-cut details inside the dark shapes, cut right through so that the white shows: roof slates as rows of tiny scallops, the joints of the stones in the cottage walls as thin cut lines, plank gaps in the jetty and the boats, hoops of the barrels and planks of the crates as thin slits, the rope coil as a spiral slit, delicate railings and rigging as fine threads of paper. The outer silhouettes stay bold, simple and readable. Not a photograph, not a 3D render, not a painting, not a postcard, no colour at all.
